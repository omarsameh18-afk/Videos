# Why don't woodpeckers get concussions?

**Idea:** Woodpeckers' skulls don't cushion their pecks at all; their heads hit like stiff hammers, and their tiny brains are what keep them safe.

## Voiceover (final, recorded: 59 words, 19.2 s)

So this woodpecker keeps banging its face into a tree.
Why doesn't its brain get hurt?
You'd think its skull's a shock absorber, right?
Nope. The whole head stops as one stiff piece, like a hammer.
A cushion would just make its pecks weaker.
The trick is size.
A tiny brain feels way less pressure from the same hit.

(First draft, 67 words: "A woodpecker hammers its face into wood again and again. ..." - replaced by the casual version above.)
