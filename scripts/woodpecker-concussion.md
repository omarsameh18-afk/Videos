# Why don't woodpeckers get concussions?

**Idea:** Woodpeckers' skulls don't cushion their pecks at all; their heads hit like stiff hammers, and their tiny brains are what keep them safe.

## Voiceover (67 words)

A woodpecker hammers its face into wood again and again. So why doesn't its brain get hurt? People assumed its skull was a built-in shock absorber. But high-speed video showed the whole head stops as one stiff unit, like a hammer. A cushion would waste energy and make drilling harder. The real protection is size. A tiny brain feels much less pressure from the same sudden stop.
